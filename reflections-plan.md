# Reflections series — plan tree

A roadmap for the **Learning & Philosophical Reflections** section. (Private planning
note; not published as a page.)

Ground rule, agreed 2026-06-12: no new post may re-argue ground the published essays
already hold. Overlap is allowed in exactly one form — **extension**: picking up a thread
an existing post explicitly planted (a quoted phrase, an open question, a one-paragraph
aside) and developing it into a full argument. Every node below therefore carries an
**Extends** anchor (the planted thread, with its source) and an **Off-limits** list
(what that post already settled and must not be re-litigated).

## Branch A — The Inner Craft

**A1. On Being Stuck.** The phenomenology of the wall: what actually happens in the
hours when nothing works, and why those hours are not waste but the workload itself.
Pólya's moves as posture rather than recipe; incubation; the difference between
productive struggle (probing a new face of the problem each attempt) and rumination
(replaying the same failed move); keeping a written ledger of failed attempts as both
therapy and method.
*Extends:* "whether you can stay steady when a problem refuses to budge"
(On Learning, the braided-forces paragraph) — named there as a force, never developed.
*Off-limits:* re-arguing that friction is the lesson (Spectator Sport owns that);
this post starts *after* you've accepted the struggle and asks how to inhabit it.
*Seeds:* Pólya, *How to Solve It*; Wiles's attic years; Tao's blog on problem-solving
strategy. Epigraph candidate: Pólya or Beckett ("Fail again. Fail better.").

**A2. Taste: the Instrument You Train Without Noticing.** Judgment was defined
(On Learning) as turning knowledge into choices; this post asks where the *criteria*
come from. Hardy's three marks of mathematical beauty (economy, unexpectedness,
inevitability) tested against the blog's own material — the two-square identity, the
inscribed-rectangle proof; taste as trained prediction ("this approach smells right");
the dark side: taste calcifies, and entire fields have been delayed by collective taste
(quaternions vs. vectors is the house example — the Kelvin "unmixed evil" fight was a
*taste* war, not a correctness war).
*Extends:* "wisdom is the ability to turn knowledge into judgment" (On Learning) and
Russell's beauty epigraph, plus the quaternion post's history section.
*Off-limits:* the intuition-vs-rigor dialectic (settled by Tao's three stages); don't
re-tell the full quaternion history, only invoke its taste-war reading.
*Seeds:* Hardy, *Apology*; von Neumann "you don't understand things, you get used to
them"; Thurston, *On Proof and Progress*.

**A3. In Praise of Forgetting.** If math is compression (On Learning's closing image),
then forgetting is the compressor. Understanding is precisely what survives forgetting:
you forget the theorem statement but keep the ability to re-derive it, and that residue
— the regeneration recipe, not the stored result — is the real unit of mathematical
knowledge. Why re-deriving beats reviewing; spaced *re-derivation* (not spaced
re-reading); the strange comfort that even Hilbert had to relearn his own work.
*Extends:* "only understanding that has actually been organized compounds" and the
compression paragraph (On Learning); also Spectator Sport's "you never owned it, you
only visited" — flipped to the long term.
*Off-limits:* the reinvention argument itself (Spectator owns first-time reinvention;
this post is about the *n*-th time).
*Seeds:* Ebbinghaus, honestly handled; "education is what remains after one has
forgotten what one has learned" (attribution swamp — flag it); Rota on "the things a
mathematician keeps."

**A4. Notation: the Names That Think for You.** The deliberate dialectical sequel to
Feynman's bird. On Learning argued the name is not the thing; this post argues the
counterpoint: a *well-chosen* name is cognitive technology that does real inferential
work. Hindu–Arabic vs. Roman numerals (the algorithm lives in the notation); Leibniz's
$dy/dx$ beating Newton's dots for a century of calculus; how $i, j, k$ had to be carved
out of quaternions before "vector" could be thought cleanly (links the quaternion post);
Iverson's "notation as a tool of thought"; when notation gaslights you (the $f(x)$
overload, $\sin^2$ vs $\sin^{-1}$).
*Extends:* Feynman's bird (On Learning) — by explicit, announced opposition; and
"concepts handed down feel like furniture in someone else's house" (Spectator Sport):
good notation is how furniture becomes yours.
*Off-limits:* "knowing the name isn't knowing the thing" must be affirmed, not rehashed
— state it in one sentence and move to the converse.
*Seeds:* Iverson's Turing lecture; Cajori, *History of Mathematical Notations*; the
quaternion→vector fossil from the quaternions post.

## Branch B — The Social Fabric

**B1. The Mentor's Sentence.** Spectator Sport gave one paragraph to the teacher who
pulls you aside; this post gives it the essay it deserves. Why a single sentence of
believed-in expectation outperforms terabytes of content; being *known* as a learning
technology (the AI tutor "forgets you completely" line, developed); study groups as
places where confusion is allowed out loud; the seminar tradition; what to do if no
mentor ever shows up (how to synthesize the sentence for yourself — the honest hard case).
*Extends:* the mentor paragraph + the *educere* etymology (Spectator Sport).
*Off-limits:* the general "bottleneck is desire" claim (already argued); assume it,
build on it.
*Seeds:* Thurston's MathOverflow answer ("What can one contribute to mathematics?");
Halmos on teachers; possibly Rosenthal/Pygmalion handled with citations and care.

**B2. Being Wrong in Public.** Welcome promised "when I get something wrong, I would
rather fix it in the open." This post is the theory of that promise. The errata culture
of mathematics; published proofs that were wrong and how the community metabolized the
errors (Wiles's gap and the year of repair as the centerpiece narrative); why math's
relationship to error is its most exportable virtue; how the fear of being wrong — not
the errors themselves — is what actually stalls learners; the blog's own corrections as
a running exhibit.
*Extends:* Welcome's correction promise; On Learning's "for shame when they should just
correct the error."
*Off-limits:* math-anxiety-as-wound (that is B3's territory; B2 is about error as
epistemic practice, B3 about emotional repair — keep the boundary explicit).
*Seeds:* the Wiles–Taylor repair; Lakatos, *Proofs and Refutations* (also feeds C2 —
decide which post gets the deep treatment; suggest C2).

**B3. The Wound and the Repair.** On Learning diagnosed in two paragraphs how bad
teaching builds "a low background hum — *math hurts me*." This post is the repair
manual: what adult relearning actually looks like; why returning learners should start
*above* where school left them (structure, not arithmetic); choosing material where the
sense (Quidditch's term, if published) gets switched on before the drill; the Woo
glasses metaphor extended into a protocol rather than a slogan.
*Extends:* the guessing-game/wound passage (On Learning); Woo's "I don't have a maths
brain" passage (Quidditch draft — if that post stays unpublished, this one absorbs and
credits its core metaphor, which resolves the draft's fate).
*Off-limits:* re-diagnosing the wound (done); this post is treatment only. No
pop-psychology grit/growth-mindset claims without flagged evidence.
*Seeds:* adult-education literature, carefully; the blog's own posts as the example
syllabus ("start with the spectral theorem picture, not with fractions").

## Branch C — The Nature of the Thing

**C1. Discovered or Invented?** The quaternions post planted it in one clause:
"discovered rather than invented." Make the case study the argument: the two-square
identity *forcing* complex multiplication, 15 *forbidding* three dimensions, Hurwitz
closing the door — discovery-flavored facts if there ever were any; then the honest
other side: notation, definitions, even "which questions count" are invented (Quidditch's
golden-ratio folklore as invented pattern). Land on a working position: the *constraints*
are discovered, the *carvings* are invented — and the interesting question is why the
carvings keep fitting the world (Wigner's unreasonable effectiveness, one section, not
the whole essay).
*Extends:* quaternions post (the forcing chain) + Quidditch's pattern-skeptic.
*Off-limits:* the full philosophy-of-math survey (no -isms tour); stay anchored to the
blog's own case studies.
*Seeds:* Wigner (1960); Hamming's reply; the quaternions/Hurwitz material already live.

**C2. What Is a Proof, Really?** Quidditch (draft) called proof "the sense's built-in
error correction" in one paragraph; Spectator Sport called for "respect for the very
idea of proof." Neither says what a proof *is*. The candid answer: a proof is both a
formal object and a social act that convinces a community (Lakatos as the engine —
*Proofs and Refutations* on the Euler characteristic, told as the story it is); what
computer-checked proofs (Four Color, Kepler/Flyspeck, Lean) change and what they don't;
where AI provers fit — one section, scoped to proof, so it extends rather than repeats
On Learning's AI argument ("a machine-found proof still leaves the human question:
does it *make you think*?").
*Extends:* the proof-as-skeptic paragraph (Quidditch draft) + the AI boundary question
(On Learning, explicitly left open there).
*Off-limits:* the general AI-in-learning debate (settled framing: think-for-you vs.
make-you-think — reuse the test, don't re-derive it).
*Seeds:* Lakatos; De Millo–Lipton–Perlis "Social Processes and Proofs"; Gonthier's
Four-Color formalization; Thurston again.

**C3. What Kind of Person? (capstone).** On Learning ends: "what kind of person do we
actually hope mathematics will make of us? … being willing to ask it, seriously, is
already a kind of beginning." This post is the asking. The virtues mathematics trains
when it goes right: honesty under constraint (you cannot bluff a proof), comfort with
prolonged not-knowing (A1's residue), reverence for reasons over authority (the
obedience line from On Learning, paid off), the habit of steelmanning (every proof
survives only if its strongest objection fails). And the failure modes when it goes
wrong: cleverness as cruelty, rigor as gatekeeping. Closes the series' arc by answering
the question the series opened with — write it last.
*Extends:* the explicit open question (On Learning's final section).
*Off-limits:* everything — this post may *summarize* every branch in a sentence each,
but its new content is the answer, not the recap.
*Seeds:* Russell's "faith in reason" passage (already quoted once — return to it
deliberately); Simone Weil on attention; Grothendieck's "rising sea" temperament.

## Branch D — The Practice (handle with care)

**D1. Problems vs. Exercises.** On Learning gave the test for worthwhile problems
("does it open onto a reusable structure?"); this post builds the taxonomy on top of
it. Halmos's "the heart of mathematics is problems"; exercises calibrate, problems
transform; how to *choose* what to work on (the skill nobody teaches); when to abandon
a problem honorably; problem-as-story (one pointer to Quidditch's narrative claim, not
a re-argument).
*Extends:* the worthwhile-problem test (On Learning) + "treat every theorem as a
challenge" (Spectator Sport, one clause there).
*Off-limits:* why struggle matters (owned); reading methods (owned by Spectator
Sport's backwards-reading section and D2 if written).
*Seeds:* Halmos; Schoenfeld on problem selection; the Putnam-vs-research contrast from
the Quidditch draft's tetrahedron passage.

**D2. The Art of Not Reading (optional — write only if it stays distinct).** Spectator
Sport owns "read the book backwards." The only non-overlapping content left in this
territory: the skill of *not* reading — skimming strategies, depth-first vs.
breadth-first through a textbook, the debt ledger (notes on what you skipped and owe),
when abandoning a book is the mature move. If a draft of this keeps drifting toward
exercises-first, kill it and fold the surviving paragraphs into D1.
*Extends:* backwards-reading (Spectator Sport), as a system rather than a habit.
*Off-limits:* the backwards-reading argument itself; reinvention.

## Voice notes

These are essays, not explainers: neither the `intuition-first-explainer` nor the
`abbott-style-analysis` register. The house essay voice (per the four published pieces):
epigraph; empathetic, second-person opening; concrete mathematical exhibits from the
blog's own posts wherever possible (the series should feel load-bearing on the rest of
the site); honest "what I'm still not sure about" sections where the evidence is thin;
no listicles. Every factual or attributed claim cited or flagged, per Welcome's promise
— the attribution swamps (Ebbinghaus folklore, Pygmalion, the "education is what
remains" quote) are called out in the nodes above on purpose.
